# The Pass — Onboarding (one shift)

You are one of the two people this thing works for. Here is everything you need
in your first shift.

## What it is, in one paragraph

An agent runs on a small computer in the corner. It watches ingredients, works
out what to order and when, keeps the cold chain honest, photographs plates as
they cross the pass, keeps a two-lane line between the kitchen and the floor, and
writes a short report at the end of every service. It never orders anything,
never approves anything and never tells you a dish is good — it drafts, it
counts, it reminds, and you decide.

## Your first hour: three boards

Open the boards (`the-pass boards --role chef`, `--role agent`, `--role server`,
or the web view):

- **Cuisine** — your ideas and dishes: R&D → Testing → On Menu → 86'd.
- **Operations** — the agent's queue. If something sits here, it is waiting on a
  human: an order to approve, a temperature problem, an order window closing
  today, a dish that is drifting.
- **Hospitality** — sections, sidework, occasions, notes for the floor.

## Your first service: the three habits

1. **Score the plates.** Photograph each dish at the pass, then tap the score on
   your phone (four numbers and an optional note). Ten seconds per plate. The
   trend is what matters — one 3 is noise, three 3s is a dish changing.
2. **Use the line properly.** Urgent means "stop and read this" (allergies, 86s,
   a plate dying). Everything else goes in the quiet lane and arrives as a digest.
   If you send urgent, you will be asked to acknowledge it — that is the feature.
3. **Read the journal before you leave.** Two minutes. It names the weakest
   plates of the night, the pace, the cold chain and the calls.

## Saying things to it

On Telegram: `86 the scallops`, `how's the pit`, `order status`,
`show me tonight`, `temps`, `reminders`, `journal`, `michelin`, `ack <id>`, `help`.

## What it will not do

- Approve an order. It will list what is waiting and tell you where to approve it.
- Claim a star is coming. It shows five criteria computed from your records and
  the weakest one to work on.
- Invent a number. If there is no history yet, it says so.

## The first week

- Day 1: define your temperature zones, add your recipes with portion sizes and
  menu prices, and enter your suppliers with their order cutoffs.
- Day 2: let it draft your order. Correct it. It learns the supplier's real
  delivery record from what you log.
- Day 3: start photographing and scoring plates. Nothing else changes the drift
  report as much.
- Day 4-7: read the journal nightly, work the drift cards, and watch which
  criterion the dashboard keeps pointing at.
